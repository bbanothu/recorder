Status: **Design approved (2026-10-02 decision round); dispatch-ready with the plan.** Design round folded in: the operator chose one
release covering both halves, **full-timeline per-user audio (no silence skipped)**, a

## Goal

Two real symptoms on the operator's 2026-10-02 session: only **one of two speakers**
reached the transcript at all, and the one who did is stamped at times **minutes earlier
than he spoke**. After this release a captured track is never silently dropped from the
transcript, and every line lands at its true meeting time.

## Root cause (verified on the operator's own artifacts, not inferred)

Evidence from session `1473835448932634708/meeting-2026-10-02-16-20-56`:

| | `billy-20261002-162056-057.ogg` | `Fragr-20261002-162102-258.ogg` |
|---|---|---|
| Opus packets libopus **rejects** | **2 — packet index 0 and 3** (the first frames of the subscription; 94 B, 99 B, TOC `0xc5`/`0xd7` = truncated multi-frame packets) | **0 of 6741** |
| ffmpeg CLI decode | recovers → 420.14 s; 3× `Error parsing the packet header`, 2× `Invalid data found` | clean → 134.82 s |
| **PyAV** decode (what faster-whisper reads with) | **decodes NOTHING** — `InvalidDataError: avcodec_send_packet()` | 134.82 s |
| whisper `small` (production sidecar) | `{"language":"en","segments":[]}` | **16 segments** (`vad_filter=True`, `beam_size=5`, CPU `int8`, last end 134.11 s; 15 without VAD) — the delivered transcript's 12 lines are a different run, not this setting |
| track duration vs. meeting | 420.04 s container = 21 002 × 20 ms | 134.82 s container = 6 741 × 20 ms — **both by construction**: `src/ogg-mux.js:132-145` advances `granulePos` by `samplesPerFrame` per packet, so container duration carries **no** wall-clock information |
| quiet stretches ≥1 s below −50 dB | **0** | **9 — 12.2912 s total, longest 2.2998 s (35.94 → 38.24 s)** |
| pages / avg page bytes / DTX-sized (≤40 B) | 21 004 / 146 B / 5.1% | 6 743 / 120 B / 15.1% |

**Defect 1 — a poison packet kills the whole track for whisper.** The master mix decodes
every packet with opusscript and silently drops the undecodable ones (`src/master-mix.js:135-139`)
— ~40 ms of garbage nobody hears, which is why the master mix sounds complete. `Recorder`
buffers the raw packets into the track *including* that garbage (`src/recorder.js:88-105`,
`:170-175`). `faster_whisper.audio.decode_audio` wraps PyAV frames in
`_ignore_invalid_frames` (`audio.py:79-88`, `except av.error.InvalidDataError: continue`),
but PyAV's decode generator is finished once it raises: the loop breaks and **everything
after the bad packet is discarded**. billy's poison packet is at index 0, so whisper
received *zero* audio. Reproduced locally: one invalid packet in an otherwise valid 4 s
track yields 2.00 s to PyAV and 3.98 s to ffmpeg.

**Defect 2 — a per-user track is not on the meeting clock.** Nothing in the recorder *cuts*
silence: the receiver subscribes with `EndBehaviorType.Manual` (`src/bot.js:241`) and Discord's
client stops transmitting while a user is quiet. What that leaves is worse than trimming — the
un-transmitted time is absent from the file, and the muxer then lays every packet down
back-to-back: `muxOpusToOgg` advances `granulePos` by `samplesPerFrame` per packet
(`src/ogg-mux.js:132-145`), so a track's container duration is exactly `packets × 20 ms` and says
nothing about wall clock. The master mix does the opposite and is the model for the fix: it
positions each packet at `Math.round((atMs - t0) / FRAME_MS)` on the wall-clock grid and leaves
unspoken time as zeros in the sparse temp (`src/master-mix.js:148-158`, class note `:40-49`).
The master is on the meeting clock; the per-user tracks are not.

Measured on the operator's 2026-10-02 session: the two participants were subscribed ~6 s apart
(16:20:56.057, 16:21:02.258) and their tracks hold 420.04 s and 134.82 s of audio. A track can
never contain more audio than its own subscription, so that session ran **at least 420 s** — and
the 134.82 s track therefore cannot be the whole meeting: **~285 s of it is not in that file at
all**. `src/transcribe.js:47` maps track time onto meeting time by addition
(`Math.max(0, track.startedAtMs + sg.start * 1000 - sessionStartMs) / 1000`), so every missing
second shifts that speaker's later lines **earlier**. The delivered transcript shows exactly
that: one speaker's lines land `[00:07]`→`[02:09]` while the other's audio fills 7 minutes of the
same call. Cross-speaker interleaving is therefore impossible to get right from these files, and
the operator's stated goal — *who said what, when* — is unachievable without preserving the gaps.

Two corrections from the audit round, because the first draft of this defect overstated its
case: (a) the wire is **not** free of near-silence — Discord does transmit low-bitrate
DTX-sized frames at times (5.1% of billy's packets and 15.1% of Fragr's are ≤40 B) and Fragr's
track genuinely holds 9 quiet stretches ≥1 s below −50 dB, so "zero quiet stretches on both
tracks" was wrong; and (b) measured quiet is beside the point — quiet *audio* is not preserved
*wall clock*, which is why the fix below fills **arrival gaps**, not measured silence.

`addUser` is called from `connection.receiver.speaking.on('start')` (`src/bot.js:408`), so a
track's `startedAtMs` is already the moment its first speech arrives: **preserving gaps
inside the track makes the existing timestamp math exact with no renderer change.**

## Actors

- **Operator** — runs the bot, receives the transcript DM; needs every speaker present at
  their true times, and needs silence about a speaker to be impossible.
- **Participant** — their audio is captured; nothing user-facing changes for them.
- **Bot process** — one session at a time; no new failure path may exit the process
  (AGENTS.md: a dead process loses live recordings).

## Key decisions

### D1 — Per-user tracks preserve the full meeting timeline (operator-approved, operator-mandated)

- Gaps between a user's packets are filled with **libopus-encoded silence frames** on the
  20 ms grid, sized from packet arrival times (the recorder already has a `now` seam). This
  mirrors the master mix's *placement* — `round((atMs - t0) / 20)` per packet with unspoken
  time left as zeros (`src/master-mix.js:148-158`) — but the per-user path must write real
  frames, because its muxer lays packets down back-to-back with a per-packet granule
  (`src/ogg-mux.js:132-145`) and would otherwise compress every gap away. Nothing is skipped;
  the track spans the whole time that user was subscribed.
- Only gaps `> GAP_THRESHOLD_MS` (strictly above 40 ms — at or below that it is jitter, not
  silence) are filled, quantized to whole 20 ms frames.
- The silence frame is one memoized libopus-encoded 20 ms zero-PCM frame (`opusscript`,
  the encoder already in the tree) **shared by reference** across every padded slot — no
  per-frame allocation. Measured: **3 bytes**, decodes to exactly 1920 zero samples.
- Measured consequence: the mux writes one packet per page, so silence costs **31 B per
  20 ms ≈ 5.3 MB per speaker-hour of silence** (a 37.4 s gap-padded probe = 71 207 B; the
  equivalent speech-only track = 16 957 B). Accepted: local disk, and the alternative
  (page-grouping many frames per Ogg page) is a documented ~10× follow-up, not this patch.
- Verified end-to-end on the padded probe: ffprobe duration **37.4 s** (the silence is in
  the timeline), ffmpeg decodes with **no errors**, all packets libopus-decodable, **PyAV
  reads the full 37.54 s**. faster-whisper's VAD restores original offsets
  (`faster_whisper/transcribe.py:1009-1010` → `restore_speech_timestamps`), so segment times
  come back as absolute positions on the padded timeline — no mapping layer needed.
- A packet dropped by D2 leaves a ≤20 ms tightening of the grid rather than a pad. Accepted
  and documented (it is dropped precisely because it is not audio).

### D2 — A track keeps only packets the decoder accepts (operator-approved)

- The verdict comes from the master mix's per-user decoder, which already decodes every
  packet: `push()` returns `false` only when libopus rejected the packet or it decoded to
  nothing, and `true` at every other exit — absent mix, `t0 == null`, `ioError`
  (`src/master-mix.js:121-140`). **A master failure must never strip tracks**; only a decode
  verdict may drop a packet. `Recorder#onData` keeps the frame unless the hook returned
  `false`; a hook **throw** keeps it (existing isolation, `src/recorder.js:95-104`).
- Reuses the one decode pass the session already performs instead of adding a second
  decoder pass over every packet; one definition of "decodable audio".
- Each entry counts dropped packets; `#finalize` reports the count, and a track whose
  frames were *all* dropped says so instead of vanishing.
- **Not** doing: resetting a user's decoder after a rejected packet (libopus recovers per
  packet; a reset would add a concealment artifact for no benefit).

### D3 — A captured track is never deleted (operator-approved)

- `#finalize`'s `confirmed` precondition goes (`src/recorder.js:159-166`). Anyone who
  emitted audio keeps their file. `SpeakingGate` is unchanged but becomes **log-only**:
  `markConfirmed` keeps its name and call site (`src/bot.js:366-368`) and its "track opened"
  log line; its stored flag is renamed `announced` so no field named `confirmed` sits next
  to a rule that no longer consults it.
- Ordering note: this lands after D1/D2 on purpose — widening which tracks exist while
  tracks could still poison a decoder would expose *more* sessions to defect 1.

### D4 — The session timeline file (operator-designed)

- New append-only `timeline-<stamp>.jsonl` in the session dir, written live (one JSON
  object per line, `appendFileSync`), so it survives a crash:
  - `session` — meeting start `t0` = the recorder's single clock read (the same value that
    feeds `master.start()`, Decision-7 discipline), guild.
    (Channel id dropped in the audit round: `createRecorder` never receives one and the
    `Recorder` constructor has no such field, so recording it would mean threading a new seam
    through `src/bot.js` for metadata the guild id and session directory already carry.)
  - `track` — when a user's file is created: `userId`, `displayName`, `file`, `t`, and the
    delta from `t0` (the operator's "start time + current time" entry).
  - `speech` — a timestamp for every talk burst: the first packet and every packet after a
    gap `> GAP_THRESHOLD_MS`, with the burst's track position `trackMs`.
  - `join` / `leave` / `reconnect` — participant lifecycle (a re-add of an id already seen,
    including a D7 stream re-subscribe).
  - `end` — duration.
- **Role, stated so nobody builds a redundant layer:** with D1 the *audio* carries the
  clock, so this file is the attribution/coverage record and the diagnostic the operator
  asked for — not a second time-mapping source. A reader must not re-derive times from it.
- Written only when per-user files are written (the `writePerUserFiles` seam covers it).
  Its own failure mode: one log line, never a throw into the voice-data callback.

### D5 — The near-empty skip counts speech, not padded frames (adopted default)

- D1 makes `frames` silence-dominated, so `MIN_TRACK_FRAMES = 100` (~2 s, `src/transcribe.js:97`)
  must be evaluated against **speech** frames or the rule stops meaning anything: a quiet
  participant's padded track would pass the threshold and run a sidecar over pure silence.
  `userFiles` entries gain `speechFrames`; the skip and its log line use it. Lands in the
  same commit as D1 (the rule's input changed meaning).

### D6 — Transcript reporting and attributions (operator-approved)

- A speaker with no recognized speech is named, never implied: one
  `_No speech recognized for **billy** (billy-….ogg)._` line per text-less track, placed
  between the header and the first entry. `_No speech detected._` stays when nothing at all
  was recognized (the pinned wording), with the per-track notes appended after it. Output is
  byte-identical to today when every track has text.
- Rejoin/reconnect events (D4) drive a coverage note when one display name spans more than
  one track, e.g. `_billy: 2 tracks in this meeting (left and rejoined at 13:20); lines are
  merged under one label._` Lines keep one label per person — no `(2nd connection)` noise
  mid-transcript.
- The stop-time summary stops counting tracks as speakers: `(16 segments, 2 tracks, 1 with
  speech)`. Per-track lines: `transcribed <file>: N segments` /
  `no speech recognized in <file> (0 segments) — audio kept`.

### D7 — A dead per-user stream is logged, survivable, re-subscribed (adopted default)

- `Recorder` installs `error` and `close` listeners on each per-user stream. Today there is
  none, and `@discordjs/voice` destroys a stream with an error on any parse/decrypt throw —
  so the process would take an unhandled `'error'` and the live recording would die.
- `addUser`'s `this.users.has(user.id)` early return makes a dead stream permanent (the id
  is present, so nothing ever re-subscribes and that speaker is silent for the rest of the
  session). It now finalises the dead entry (keeping the audio captured so far) and opens a
  fresh subscription, logged as a `reconnect` in the timeline.

### D8 — The transcription read path canonicalises before the sidecar (operator-approved)

- `runTranscription` (`src/transcribe.js:99-117`) transcodes the input to **16 kHz mono s16
  WAV** with ffmpeg first and runs the sidecar on that, unlinking the temp in a `finally`.
  ffmpeg's CLI recovers past an undecodable packet; PyAV does not, so the fragile container
  never reaches it. This is the only fix that repairs **already-recorded** sessions on a
  re-run, and it is immunity against the next unknown decoder quirk.
- Decode errors ffmpeg reports are counted and logged (one line per track) so recovery is
  visible instead of silent. ffmpeg missing/failing → run the sidecar on the raw track
  (today's behaviour) plus one log line: a transcode problem must never cost the transcript.
- Temp `<track-base>.transcribe.tmp.wav` in the session dir (same volume as the recording);
  `src/tmp-cleanup.js`'s startup sweep gains that suffix, so a killed process leaves no
  residue. Measured cost: **909 ms** for the 420 s track (≈460× realtime), 12.8 MB temp at
  16 kHz mono s16 (115 MB per track-hour).
- Considered and rejected: piping ffmpeg's stdout into the sidecar's stdin (a two-child
  protocol for no benefit — faster-whisper materialises the whole array anyway), and
  re-decoding inside `scripts/transcribe.py` (untestable in this node suite, and it would
  leave the node path — the one production uses — unfixed).

### D9 — Release mechanics (AGENTS.md, non-negotiable)

- `package.json` `3.0.1` → **`3.0.2`** with a matching `## [3.0.2] - <date>` CHANGELOG
  heading. README (Transcription + Known limits: tracks now carry the full timeline; the
  timeline file; the no-silence-on-the-wire truth; the decode-recovery log line) and SETUP
  (ffmpeg is now also used by the transcription path) updated in the release commit. No env
  var changes, so `.env.example` is untouched.
- Audio change ⇒ measured numbers, not estimates (AGENTS.md testing checklist): the silence
  frame size, the per-page silence cost, and the transcode cost are recorded in the spec's
  Measurements table and re-verified in the plan's release task.

## Out of scope

- The master mix's own silence/PLC quality and `MASTER_BITRATE` tuning.
- The two written-but-undispatched specs (v3.1.0-durability, v3.2.0-streaming-encode): this
  release ships first; they keep their numbers (3.0.2 < 3.1.0, so no renumbering).
- Retention/cleanup policy, `/status`, the streaming encode.
- Page-grouping silence frames to cut padding ~10× (measured potential) — a documented
  follow-up if disk use ever matters.
- Deleting or rewriting existing recordings; D8 makes a manual re-transcribe work instead.
- **Pre-existing, filed separately (not this patch):** the sidecar's CUDA gate
  (`scripts/transcribe.py:16`, `:26-31`) reports CUDA present whenever
  `ctranslate2.get_cuda_device_count() > 0`, so on a host that reports a device but lacks
  `cublas64_12.dll` the sidecar dies with `RuntimeError` before loading the model. It is why
  the whisper rows above had to be reproduced on CPU; it needs its own patch release
  (v3.0.3 candidate) and is out of v3.0.2's scope.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC1** A track built from frames that include a libopus-rejected packet never gains that
  packet — the written file holds exactly the decoder-accepted set, asserted through the
  **production hook** (`src/bot.js`'s `onPacket` returning the mix's verdict) as well as
  through the recorder's own hook seam — and the drop is counted and logged once per track.
  The decode-level proof sits one level down: AC11's fixture harness decodes the produced file
  with ffmpeg/PyAV, and Task 8 re-runs the operator's real track end to end.
- **AC2** `MasterMix.push()` returns `false` only for a packet its decoder rejected (or
  decoded to nothing), and `true` when the mix is absent, not started, or in `ioError`.
- **AC3** A track recorded across a gap in packet arrivals decodes to a duration that
  includes the gap (measured on the written file, not on an in-memory array), and the pad
  frames are the shared silence frame.
- **AC4** `userFiles` entries carry `speechFrames`; the near-empty skip fires on
  `speechFrames < 100` and a long-but-silent track is skipped with its existing log line.
- **AC5** The timeline file is append-only JSONL containing session, track, speech,
  join/leave/reconnect and end events with the documented fields; `track.t` minus
  `session.t0` equals the delta recorded in the event; a write failure logs once and never
  throws into the voice-data callback.
- **AC6** The timeline reader skips a corrupt line without throwing (the reader lands in
  Task 6; Task 4 cannot demonstrate this, so the clause belongs here, not in AC5); a speaker
  whose track yields no segments gets one `_No speech recognized for …_`
  line between header and entries; `_No speech detected._` survives the all-empty case; a
  display name spanning two tracks gets one rejoin note; when every track has text the
  rendered markdown is byte-identical to today's output.
- **AC7** The summary logs `(<segments> segments, <tracks> tracks, <speech> with speech)`;
  the old "N speakers" phrasing is gone; per-track segment counts are logged.
- **AC8** A track captured but never confirmed by the gate is written, listed in `userFiles`,
  and reaches the sidecar.
- **AC9** A per-user stream emitting `'error'` or `'close'` does not throw, is logged once,
  marks the entry dead, finalises the audio captured so far, and a later `addUser` for the
  same id opens a new subscription (recorded as a `reconnect`) and records again.
- **AC10** `runTranscription` invokes ffmpeg (16 kHz mono s16 WAV) **before** the sidecar,
  passes the WAV path, removes the temp on success and on sidecar failure, counts ffmpeg's
  decode errors into one log line, and falls back to the raw track (one log line) when
  ffmpeg is unusable.
- **AC11** On the committed poison-packet fixture, PyAV decodes nothing from the raw track
  and the full duration from the canonicalised artifact — the bash harness skips with a loud
  notice when python+PyAV are unavailable (the dev host may lack them; the image has them).
- **AC12** `package.json` `3.0.2` + matching CHANGELOG heading + README/SETUP updates in the
  release commit; `npm test` green (293 + new, 0 fail); the 4 bash cases green; the golden
  fixture `test/fixtures/master-mix-golden.wav` byte-unchanged.

## Risks

- **Whisper over long silence:** a padded track is mostly silence. Mitigation is already in
  place — `scripts/transcribe.py:19` runs `vad_filter=True` and faster-whisper restores
  original offsets, so silence is pruned without shifting any timestamp. D5 keeps
  genuinely quiet participants from reaching the sidecar at all. The release task re-runs
  one real transcript end-to-end to confirm times land at the expected minutes.
- **Timestamp skew from the transcode (D8):** measured +0.10 s on billy's 420 s track
  (420.14 s WAV vs 420.04 s container). Below the transcript's 1 s display granularity;
  recorded so a later reader does not mistake it for drift.
- **`push()`'s return value becomes a contract** between mix and recorder: the
  absent/not-started/`ioError` branches must keep returning "keep", or a master failure
  would silently strip every track. AC2 pins all four branches.
- **Disk/time cost:** measured 5.3 MB per speaker-hour of silence, 909 ms transcode per
  420 s track, 115 MB temp per track-hour. Stated in README, not hidden.
- **The bash harness skips on a host without python+PyAV.** AC1/AC3/AC10's node assertions
  are the portable gate; the harness is the end-to-end proof and runs in the image where
  transcription happens.
